Let's go

Southwest Desert Hiking

National Parks are one of our favorite destinations, and we often try to include a couple in the same trip when possible. In November 2026, we planned and grouped hikes in Joshua Tree National Park, Grand Canyon West, and Death Valley National Park with a few one-off trips in between and around along the way. We start in Las Vegas, make our way to Joshua Tree National Park with a day trip to San Bernadino and Palm Springs included, then travel to Grand Canyon West for a night before moving on back to Vegas to pickup another traveler and finish our adventure in Death Valley National Park before a flight home out of Las Vegas one final time.

Joshua Tree, NOV 11th 2026

We started from Las Vegas taking advantage of a direct flight, burned some time at Shark Reef aquarium and ate some brunch at Mandalay Bay before driving southwest and grabbing some groceries on our way into Joshua Tree to start our trip off.

Our hiking and exploring adventure started with the visitors center to review our planned day of hikes, check closures, and buy some swag. To beat the sun and heat, we started with Fortynine Palms Oasis hike, then the Cholla Cactus Garden quick loop, and finished with the Barker Dam and Hidden Valley trails.

The Fortynine Palms Oasis Trail was a great intro; it's a moderate, ~3-mile (3.2-mile) out-and-back hike in the northern section of Joshua Tree National Park near Twentynine Palms, California. We enjoyed some small climbs over a rocky ridge and descended into a hidden canyon with a spring-fed fan palm oasis for a quick look and break before making our way back. It wasn’t too hot in the morning but by the time we hiked out, the temps were rising.

Next up was Cholla Cactus Garden Trail, a flat, easy 0.25-mile loop through a 10-acre stand of dense teddy bear cholla ("jumping" cholla) cacti in the Pinto Basin. Short hike but fun seeing a forest of cholla all in one spot. Watchning the wrens fly in and out gave a little more magic to the area.

We finished our hikes with Barker Dam and Hidden Valley trails, both providing some nice rock formations and assorted flora as we made our way in and out of shade. Barker Dam, also known as Big Horn Dam, is a dam constructed by early cattlemen with water-storage reservoir located in Joshua Tree National Park in California. A heavy rain a few weeks back allowed for us to see more water than typical for the time of year. Right next door, we wrapped the day with Hidden Valley one-mile loop trail that winds among massive boulders through what was believed to be a legendary cattle rustlers' hideout. It is one of the most popular and scenic hiking trails in Joshua Tree National Park. The area is also a popular rock-climbing area, which we saw and heard a few groups enjoying some climbing.

Joshua Tree, NOV 12th 2026

Our second day in the park was really a bonus considering we hit so many of our desired hiking day one. We took the afternoon to drive to San Bernardino National Forest and stop in Palm Springs. We took some steep mountain drive towards and through Big Bear, and accidentally missed out planned morning hike. We moved on to Palm Springs where we split up for some shopping and main strip walking, whild others of us went on to hike Andreas Canyon loop (Palm trees and small stream oasis walk next to painted canyon walls). We spotted some lizards and birds as we made our way around the loop and had a quick snack before heading back.

After a long day of driving and a few upset stomachs, we chilled at our Airbnb taking in the quiet atmosphere and vibbed to another beautiful sunset. Really glad we had a nice spot with outdoor seating both close and just far enough from the park. Relaxing views and hot showers are always a bonus part of good hiking trip.

Grand Canyon West, NOV 13th 2026

Our way to Death Valley included a stop to pick up another companion. We took advantage of the extra travel time needed and stopped overnight at The Hualapai West Rim—officially known as Grand Canyon West, a visitor area owned and operated by the sovereign Hualapai Tribe in northwestern Arizona. Located about 2 to 2.5 hours from Las Vegas, it offers a different experience from the National Park’s South Rim because it is built on tribal land and operates independently. We loved our cabin stay, even though rain kept us secluded inside and to the porch most of the night. We watched a movie and the window trying to catch a break to see some star before calling it a night.

The next morning we had time for a quick tour of the skywalk and viewpoints before moving on. Eagle Point and Guano Point gave us awesome vantages into the canyon and river below. Since it was early, we were some of the only people out, which was especially nice for the skywalk. We then traveled back to Las Vegas, had some lunch at Fogo de Chão Brazilian Steakhouse and some quick walking around before the couple hour drive into Death Valley.

Death Valley National Park, NOV 15th 2026

D eath Valley National Park straddles eastern California and Nevada, and gave us both hiking and scenic drives from our home base at Death Valley Ranch Resort, with bonus highlights including seeing a few Star Wars filming locations too. It’s known for Titus Canyon, with a ghost town and colorful rocks, and Badwater Basin’s salt flats, North America's lowest point. Above, Telescope Peak Trail weaves past pine trees. North of the spiky salt mounds known as the Devil’s Golf Course, rattlesnakes live in Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes. Lots to explore.

Day one we hiked Golden Canyon / Gower Gulch / Badlands Loop (5-7 miles combined), starting at Zabriskie Point with the Badlands Loop into Gower Gulch and around to Golden Canyon to loop back. Cooler morning tempartures and clouds gave us some pleasant breezes and some shade. After that we did a quick 1/2 mile out and back walkout onto the Badwater Basin Salt Flats to peep some cool formations and take some photos. We ended our outing with Artist Palette, a scenic drive with a viewpoint stop off before heading in for shower and some much earned roasted chicken, salmon, and some ice cream and souvenirs shopping after.

Death Valley National Park, NOV 16th 2026

Our second day started with Salt Creek Interpretive Trail. Death Valley has a few specific spots where you can see the rare, endemic Death Valley pupfish surviving in extreme desert waters. Saw a few groups of them swimming and feeding off algae. We saw a few groups with a bunch swimming. Also saw fresh prints in the mud from a heron or egret, kit fox, and possibly a bobcat amongst other small bird tracks. Not surprising since it’s one of the only spots with moving water part of the year.

After our pupfish adventure, we checked out Mesquite Sand Dunes for a fun walk out onto the dunes with some interesting animal tracks, a beetle, a lizard, and countless mysterious holes in the sand. Had a great time hiking the dunes to take in the views and just enjoy the wonder of the area. Later in the day, the wind was whipping, so we hit it at a good time!

With the dunes and wind behind us, we hiked Mosaic Canyon, choosing to walk in until it was a bit too tough to scramble and turned back. Fun walk, but quite a bit of elevation gain that snuck up on your lungs. A bit bittersweet knowing it was our last true hike of the trip, but also knew we had a few drives to make still, and could always stargaze later if the clouds agreed.

We had planned to drive north to Ubehebe Craters, but with a road closure, we instead stopped at Stovepipe Wells gift shop and moved on to our final viewpoint, Dante’s View. Another Star Wars shot (overlook of Mos Eisley) and some cold winds (went from 76 to 56 in just a couple minutes of driving up) left us satified before a final 35 min drive back to the ranch for some dinner and relaxation before our next day checkout.
